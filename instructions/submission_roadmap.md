## PS1 Submission Roadmap

Move the same research project through a conference-style cycle. All dates are in 2026 and all deadlines use Beijing time UTC+8.

## Sunday September 13 by 11 PM

Submit v1 on Canvas: compiled PDF, Overleaf source with editable teaser, code snapshot, and working GitHub, Colab and Hugging Face links. Keep the submitted version before revising.

## Monday September 14 in class

Review two assigned proposals and receive two reviews. Proposed practice block: 4:00–4:10 calibration using the class LLM example; 4:10–4:30 first review; 4:30–4:50 second review; 4:50–5:05 clarification; 5:05–5:15 submit reviews and record a revision priority.

## Wednesday September 16 by 11 PM proposed

Submit one author response covering both reviews. Explain what you will revise, retain or defer, supported by evidence. A concise response of about 500–750 words is usually enough; this is guidance, not a minimum.

## Thursday September 17 by 11 PM proposed

Reviewers read the author response and add a brief follow-up on the main concern. No score negotiation or numerical re-rating is required.

## Sunday September 20 by 11 PM proposed

Submit v2 and its updated sources, code snapshot and artifact links, together with the author response. Retain v1 and the reviews. Update the cumulative reflection and Appendix E; identify the final code commit and the checks actually completed.

## Final instructor assessment

Prof. Luyao Zhang assesses the research portfolio and the complete contribution to review, response and revision. The first draft and Monday review dates are confirmed; the later dates above are proposed. No final grading date is set.

Instructor preparation: assign two papers outside each student’s workshop team, avoid reciprocal pairs and other conflicts, and provide substitute feedback if a student receives no review. With 21 students, the allocation produces 42 reviews.
