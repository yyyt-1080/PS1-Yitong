## PS1 Review and Grading Guide

Read the proposal as a developing contribution. Help the author understand what works, what needs attention and what could be communicated more clearly.

## Three questions for every review

1 What do you appreciate? Identify strengths.

2 What do you criticize constructively? Identify the most useful improvement.

3 What questions should the author consider to improve scientific communication?

## How to review

Review your two assigned proposals independently. Read the main paper, examine the teaser and try one relevant notebook or demo check. Use the three prompts on the form; support comments with a concrete location or example and say what you actually checked. One consequential improvement is more useful than a long list of minor edits.

Distinguish a necessary correction from an optional extension. Ask for work feasible within PS1. A thoughtful proposal with a small verified baseline can earn full class credit without a completed conference-scale study. Reviews are named, respectful and shared within the class. Disclose a conflict to the instructor so the paper can be reassigned.

## How to respond

Address substantive points from both reviewers. Explain what you changed, retained or deferred and why. Give the relevant page, section, figure or notebook cell and the evidence behind your decision. Reasoned disagreement is welcome. After revising, record what you reran and what happened; update Appendix E.

Reviewers then read the response and briefly explain whether their main concern was resolved. Peer comments inform the process; Prof. Luyao Zhang assigns the final grade. No numerical peer score is required.

## What to use

Use the two-page Review and Response Forms. The separate Scaffolding Companion contains optional examples and guiding questions. The Submission Roadmap gives the dates. These are aids to judgment, not additional review fields.

## How PS1 is graded

PS1 totals 100 points and contributes 20% of the course grade. The four allocations remain:

Research portfolio — 60 points. A coherent contribution, sound reasoning and checkable evidence, communicated through the paper, code and game.

Peer review — 15 points. Two specific, constructive reviews and a thoughtful follow-up after the author responds.

Author response — 10 points. Substantive answers, evidence and defensible decisions about the feedback.

Revision — 15 points. Meaningful improvement or justified retention, verification of affected artifacts and a clear account of intellectual development.

## Stellar Scholarship Threshold

“shoot for the moon, then even if you fail, you lie among the stars”

The instructor gives the research portfolio one overall quality score Q from 0 to 100, considering the contribution, reasoning and evidence, and scientific communication. A complete portfolio with a technically sound, rerunnable baseline qualifies for the threshold conversion: research points = 60 × min(Q ÷ 70, 1). Thus Q = 70 or higher earns all 60 research points; Q = 56 earns 48.

Missing required artifacts or a materially incorrect or unreproducible central baseline must be addressed before the threshold applies. Until then, research points are 0.60 × Q, capped at 59. Full PS1 credit also requires the 40 process points. Award ambition itself is not graded; a credible path to contribution is.

For each process allocation, absent work earns no points, partly supported work earns partial credit, and a complete, thoughtful contribution earns full credit. The instructor explains material deductions. This simplified guide replaces the earlier six-criterion peer scoring sheet.
